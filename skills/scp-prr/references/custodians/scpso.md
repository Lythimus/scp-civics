# St. Charles Parish Sheriff's Office (SCPSO)

Read 2026-11-02 from the office's page, https://www.stcharlessheriff.org/95/Public-Records-and-Legal-Affairs . Statute text pulled from legis.la.gov the same day. Re-read both before relying on them.

SCPSO does not use the parish form. There is no portal, so there is no form fill and no tracking number.

## Tell the user before drafting

**Copies cost money here, unlike most digital parish records.** Paid copies can add up quickly: 100 pages cost $100. Inspection is free, so offer it as the alternative.

| Item (the office's posted schedule) | Charge |
|---|---|
| Copies of documents | $1 per page |
| Audio or video recordings | $1 per minute, plus $10 per computer disk |
| Photos | $10 (the page does not say per what) |
| Postage for records sent by certified mail | Actual cost |

- The page does not separate paper from electronic copies, and R.S. 44:32(C)(1)(a) lets a custodian's fees cover "the transmission of electronic copies". Assume $1 per page for electronic copies unless the office says otherwise.
- The custodian may ask for payment before producing, and need not provide copies while the requester has an unpaid balance from an earlier request (same paragraph).
- That paragraph allows free or reduced copies for indigent citizens. The "public purpose" reduction appears in the state agency paragraph, (C)(2)(a), so do not promise it here.

## How to send it

The page accepts a written request that specifically identifies the records, by mail or in person. It says "a request will not be deemed received if transmitted by any other methods". Follow that rather than arguing with it.

| Route | Where |
|---|---|
| Mail | Captain Maurice Bostick, St. Charles Parish Sheriff's Office, P.O. Box 426, Hahnville, LA 70057 |
| In person | Captain Maurice Bostick or Administrative Assistant Rhonda Walker, 260 Judge Edward Dufresne Parkway, Luling, LA 70070. Listed hours: Monday to Friday, 8:00 am to 4:30 pm |
| Questions | Legal Affairs Division, 985-783-6237 |

- **Use certified mail with return receipt,** or hand-deliver and ask for a date-stamped copy. Mail adds transit time before the clock starts, so hand delivery is faster when a date matters.
- **Email is a courtesy copy only.** It can go alongside the mailed letter, but do not count days from it. An older `recordsrequest@` address appears in a 2017 letter found online and is not on the current page, so do not rely on it.
- **Count from receipt.** Day 1 is the first business day after the date on the return receipt or stamped copy. Exclude weekends and legal holidays (R.S. 1:55).
- **Who the custodian is.** R.S. 44:1(A)(3) defines the custodian as the head of the public body or a representative specifically authorized to respond. The Sheriff is Greg Champagne (office site; parish legal notices, June 2026). The page directs requests to Capt. Bostick, so address the letter there.

## Where to inspect records for free

R.S. 44:32(C)(3): "No fee shall be charged to any person to examine or review any public records", with exceptions stated in that Section.

| Records | Where |
|---|---|
| Initial Incident Reports and Motor Vehicle Crash Investigative Reports | Records Division, Hahnville Court House, 3rd Floor, 15045 River Road, Hahnville. These can also be requested by a letter mailed to Capt. Bostick |
| Other public records | The page does not say where they can be viewed, only that a request can be submitted in person at the Luling office. The custodian sets the day, time and place, and the model letter asks for them |

- Examination happens during regular working hours. After-hours examination, if the custodian authorizes it, requires paying staff compensation in advance (R.S. 44:32(A)(1), and the office's page).
- The custodian may ask for age and identification and may require signing a register, but may not ask why (R.S. 44:32(A)(1)). Bring ID.
- Unestablished: the Records Division's hours, and whether phone photos are allowed during inspection. Ask at the visit or call first.

## What can be withheld

Sheriffs are covered by R.S. 44:3. Read the current text for any class the request touches.

| Provision | What it covers | Drafting effect |
|---|---|---|
| 44:3(A)(1) | Pending or reasonably anticipated criminal litigation, until adjudicated or settled | Ask whether a matter is closed before expecting its file |
| 44:3(A)(2), (A)(5), 44:3(B) | Confidential sources, undercover officers | Do not expect these |
| 44:3(A)(3), 44:3.1 | Security procedures, investigative techniques, investigative technical equipment or instructions on its use, internal security information | Keep operational detail in its own numbered item, so a denial does not touch contracts, payments or policies |
| 44:3(A)(4) | Arrest records are withheld until conviction or a guilty plea, but **the initial report is public**, as are booking records, summons or citation records, and bills of information | Ask for the "initial report" by that name. The statute lists its contents: narrative, suspects charged or arrested, time, date, location, property, vehicles, investigating officers. Follow-up reports are not covered |
| 44:3(A)(8), 44:3(I) | Body-worn camera recordings | A request must be **incident-specific**, with reasonable specificity as to date, time, location or persons, or it may be denied. Recordings found to violate a reasonable expectation of privacy need a court order under R.S. 44:35 |
| 44:3(J) | Identifying details of certain victims | Expect redaction |
| 44:3(K) | A victim or designated family member may review and copy records of the offense, unless the agency certifies actual or reasonably anticipated criminal litigation | Ask whether the user is the victim. If so, say so in the letter, since this is a separate route |

**The office's own position.** Its page says that, other than the initial report, criminal offense records "are generally considered privileged" until adjudicated. It lists classes it treats as privileged even after adjudication, such as investigative reports, supplemental reports and statements, releasable only by court order. That list is the office's statement and is broader in places than the statute's wording. Tell the user what to expect, and do not argue it in a first letter.

Inmates requesting their own medical records must send the page's HIPAA form with an original signature.

Unestablished: how courts treat pending versus closed matters, and how they read "investigative technical equipment". `bayou:la-court-opinions` was not run for this file. If the request depends on either, run it, or tell the user this was not researched.

## Drafting guidelines

- **A response may come from outside counsel.** Copy counsel on later letters and quote any file number they assign.
- **Date every letter,** so the request has a date apart from the date of receipt.
- **Ask for records, not answers.** The Act does not require a custodian to answer questions or respond to argument. To get more, send a new, dated request by mail or in person, and list what was already produced so those pages are not billed again.
- **Records a vendor holds.** A custodian may say it does not maintain them. Ask for what the office itself holds, such as agreements, copies it received or exported, and its written directions to the vendor.
- **Exemptions.** Ask up front for the legal basis under R.S. 44:32(D), and for the releasable part of any record under 44:32(B).

## Model letter

Replace every bracket. Numbered items name a record type, a subject and a date range. Put them in the order the user most wants them, because the per-item estimate lets them drop items.

```
[Mailing date, written out]

Via certified mail, return receipt requested

Captain Maurice Bostick
St. Charles Parish Sheriff's Office
P.O. Box 426
Hahnville, LA 70057

Re: Public records request: [short subject]

Dear Captain Bostick:

Under the Louisiana Public Records Act, La. R.S. 44:1 et seq., I request copies of the following records of the St. Charles Parish Sheriff's Office:

1. [Record type] concerning [subject], from [date] to [date].
2. [Record type] concerning [subject], from [date] to [date].

[If an earlier request was answered:] Not requested again. I already have [records and production date]. Please do not copy them again.

Format and fees. Please provide electronic copies by email where the records exist electronically. Before making copies, please tell me the page count and the cost for each numbered item. If the total will exceed $[amount], please wait for my approval. I am also willing to inspect the records in person at no charge (R.S. 44:32(C)(3)); please tell me a day and time.

Withheld or absent records. If you withhold any record or part of a record, please release the rest (R.S. 44:32(B)) and give the written determination and legal basis that R.S. 44:32(D) requires. If a record is not in the Office's custody or control, please provide the written certificate that R.S. 44:34 requires.

Response. Please respond within five days of your receipt of this letter, not counting Saturdays, Sundays and legal public holidays, by producing the records or by sending a written determination or an estimate of the time needed.

Sincerely,

[Name]
[Return mailing address]
[Phone]
[Email]
```

Letters need a return address, since replies can come by mail. It does not have to be a home address, and a letter can resurface in later productions, so ask if the profile holds only a street address the user may prefer not to use.

## Hand-back for this custodian

- There are no portal emails. Replies come by mail or email, possibly from outside counsel, so watch both.
- Give the send steps: sign, keep a copy, mail certified with return receipt, keep the receipt.
- Calendar five business days from the return-receipt date, once it is known.
- Repeat the cost warning and the free inspection locations.
