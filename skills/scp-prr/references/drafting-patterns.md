# What makes a request fast or slow

## General pattern

These are tendencies, not parish policy, and an imminent public hearing or a large volume of records can change the result.

- **Faster:** an existing, named file; a few numbered record types; a short date range; named people; a small set of unique search terms; a dated event with a stated priority order.
- **Slower:** "all records relating to" or "not limited to"; whole offices or "any officials"; ranges of many years; no search terms; requests for legal opinions, which invite privilege review; whole-mailbox results that sweep in newsletters.

A council member said at a public meeting on 2025-12-15 that each department produces its records and that they then go to Legal for exemption review. It is an inference, but it suggests that every extra email is another item to review, so volume drives delay more than the search does.

Guidance from other states points the same way. Virginia's FOIA Council advises calling first and learning record titles ([Best Practices, 2026](https://foiacouncil.dls.virginia.gov/ref/Best%20Practices%20for%20Making%20Requests%20for%20Public%20Records%202026.html)). Montana warns that long lists of search terms, names or addresses can slow a request ([opir.mt.gov](https://opir.mt.gov)).

## Examples

These are made up, to show the difference in scope.

| Too broad | Better |
|---|---|
| All records relating to the road project | The contract, change orders and final inspection report for the [road] repair, from [date] to [date] |
| All emails about drainage complaints | Emails of [name, title] and [name, title] containing "[street name]" or "[project name]", from [date] to [date] |
| Everything the parish has on the permit application | The application, staff report and conditions for [case number] |
| Why did the department choose this vendor? | The bid tabulation and the recommendation memo for [bid number] |
| All reports about the incident | The initial report for the incident at [location] on [date] |

## Choosing a shape

- **One narrow request:** a known document or small file.
- **Part 1 and Part 2:** an existing file plus communications. Say that Part 1 records should be immediately available if they sit in a pending file, and ask for rolling production of Part 2 in a stated order, so the slow half does not hold up the fast half.
- **Separate requests:** when one part is likely to be slow, contested or privileged, or when a date matters. Each gets its own number and clock. The parish has split requests itself.

## Writing it

- Name record types and titles: application, staff report, contract, permit, invoice, inspection report. Learn the names from agendas and earlier productions. A short call to the custodian's office can help.
- Give every item a date range, and keep it as short as the question allows.
- Name people and offices for communications. Use published official addresses, and do not guess one. A name and title is enough if the address is unknown.
- Keep to 5 to 9 search terms. Prefer case numbers, project names and company names. A common word by itself brings back noise, including newsletters.
- Consider excluding mass mailings and newsletters. Asking for only the latest message in a thread reduces volume, but the skipped renderings are where different redactions show up, so let the user choose.
- Put attorney records in their own last item or a separate request.
- If a date matters, state it in one sentence with the event, then give a priority order.
- If text messages or personal accounts matter, say so and cite R.S. 44:1(A)(2)(a) after checking the current text. Whether they are searched routinely is not established, so ask expressly.
- Stay courteous and exact. Staff decide how hard to look.

## Model text

Fill the brackets and adapt it. Check the citations against the current statute first.

```
[DATE]. Under the Louisiana Public Records Act, La. R.S. 44:1 et seq., I request copies of the following records.

Part 1: [Case or file name and number]. 1. The [application], with its attachments and exhibits. 2. The [staff report] dated [date]. 3. Any [study or report type] submitted by or for [applicant].

Part 2: Communications. Custodians: [Name, title]; [Name, title]. Date range: [start] through [end]. Records: emails, letters and calendar entries of those custodians concerning [specific subject], including communications with [named company or agency]. Search terms: [term]; [term]; [term]. Please exclude newsletters and mass mailings.

Timing: [Event] is scheduled for [date]. The Part 1 records form a pending file and should be immediately available under La. R.S. 44:33(B)(1). Please produce Part 2 on a rolling basis, beginning with [priority].

If any record or part of a record is withheld, please produce the rest and give the written reasons and legal basis required by La. R.S. 44:32(D). Electronic copies are preferred.
```

Add this only if paper, video, media or GIS products are involved: `Please send a written estimate before any charge above $[amount].`

## Check before handing back

Flag and fix, or tell the user why each was kept:

- "Any and all", "all records", "not limited to", or "relating to" standing in for a record type
- An item with no date range
- Communications with no named people or no search terms
- Over 9 search terms, or terms that are common words
- A question or a request for an explanation instead of a record
- Argument, background, or the user's theory of the case
- Attorney records mixed in with other items
- No date line
- A demand or threat of suit in a first request
- A deadline stated as if it were an expedited right
- Markdown or layout that will not survive flattening into one paragraph
- Records already posted or already produced
- Personal details of anyone other than the requester
