# How a resident takes part

Checked 2026-11-17. This is not legal advice. Rules are quoted from Legistar attachments and minutes. Charter sections are paraphrased (read them with `code_search.py get`). Statutes come from legis.la.gov.

Rules change, and chairs apply them. Before a meeting that matters, confirm the current rule with the body's staff, and record who said it and when.

## Speaking at the Parish Council: two routes

The Council Rules (Legistar 2024-0004, adopted 2024-01-08) give two routes. People often confuse them.

**1. On an item set for public hearing.** This covers ordinances and resolutions on the agenda, including Planning and Zoning petitions.
- The rules' "Public Hearing Participation" page: "Persons wishing to speak in favor of or in opposition to ordinances and/or resolutions (including Planning and Zoning petitions) scheduled for public hearing shall be allowed three (3) minutes."
- The rules state no advance request for this route.
- The minutes record "Public comment opened" item by item. State law requires a comment period before the vote on any agenda item (R.S. 42:14(D)), and "reasonable rules and restrictions" on it are allowed.
- The Charter gives every interested person a chance to be heard at an ordinance's hearing (IV.B.3.c, paraphrased).

**2. "Persons to Address the Council."** This is item 9 in Rule 8's order of business, for a subject that isn't on the agenda.
- **Written request, at least a week ahead.** The Charter (VII.I) requires a written request stating the subject, received by the Council Secretary at least one week before the meeting.
- **Limits added 2024-01-08.** The Council amended the rules' first paragraph. The request must contain "the detailed nature of the subject to be discussed which must be germane to the Powers and Functions of the Parish Council and Government. No person shall be permitted to address the Council on the same subject matter upon which he /she has previously addressed the Council during the prior eight week period" (minutes of 2024-01-08).
- **Time.** "Each person may speak once no longer than five (5) minutes except with two-thirds consent of the Council, an additional three (3) minutes may be granted to either individuals or groups."
- **Groups.** A group's representative must name the group in the request and furnish a letter signed by a majority of its board, giving permission, the date of that board meeting and the subject. The group must also file a state-certified charter and an annual list of officers with the Council Secretary.
- Whether the Charter's requirement of a written request reaches route 1 hasn't been tested here. The rules apply it only to route 2, and the minutes show comment taken on hearing items without a prior letter.

**Conduct.** Rule 38 bars "personal, impertinent, slanderous or profane remarks" and disorderly conduct; the Chairman may have security remove a person. Rule 37 confines the audience, press included, to the audience section and the lobbies. Rule 9 lets the Chairman stop discussion that is "repetitious, redundant, not germane or completed".

**Written comment.** Rule 33: "Any communication which is pertinent to a pending Ordinance may be attached by the Secretary to that Ordinance and may be read before voting thereon." Send it to the Council Secretary, name the matter's file number, and ask that it be attached under Rule 33. "May" means neither the attachment nor the reading is promised. Rule 39 says mail and e-mail to the Council Office are opened and copied for the public record, except envelopes marked personal or confidential, which go to the member unopened. So a comment sent to the Council Office becomes a public record.

**Budget hearings.** The Charter gives every parish resident the right to speak at the three budget hearings (V.E, paraphrased).

## Getting something before the Council

- **Only a member or the President can introduce an ordinance** (Charter IV.B.3.b). A resident's route is to ask a member to sponsor it: their district member or either at-large member. `government.py district` names them.
- **Deadlines.** Proposed ordinances and resolutions are due to the Council Secretary by 4 PM on the sixth business day before the meeting; committee reports by noon on the fourth (Rule 34).
- **Persons to Address** (route 2 above) puts a subject before the Council but produces no vote. Rule 17: "Items that do not require Council action are placed on the agenda for information only and shall not be read at Council meetings."
- **Committees.** The Legislative Committee is the one that meets regularly (`structure.md`). Ask the sponsor whether an item will go there first.
- **Initiative petition** (below), if the Council won't act.

## Appointments and vacancies on boards

- The Council fills a vacancy on any agency it created within 30 days, by five votes (Charter III.A.6.c.iv).
- Rule 17: "All nominations for appointments shall lie over until the next meeting of the Council". A nominee's confirmation questionnaire is due to the Council Secretary by 4 PM on the fourth business day before the meeting, or the nomination is disqualified.
- Rule 40, added 2024-01-08: nominations can't be closed until each member present "has had a sufficient opportunity to submit an individual to be nominated".
- Seats, terms and appointing authority are on the parish Boards & Commissions pages (the sources named in `officials.md`). Appointments appear as Rule 8 item 12 on Council agendas, so `scp-civics:scp-watch` with the board's name catches them.

## Petitions and elections

**Initiative** (Charter VI.A, paraphrased)
- Voters may propose passage or repeal of an ordinance. It can't be used for appropriations, salaries, or repealing or reducing a tax.
- The proposer submits the proposal, and the Council specifies the petition form promptly.
- Signatures of 15% of the parish's registered voters are due within 60 days, each with date and address, and each circulator signs an affidavit.
- The Council canvasses within 30 days, and a deficient petition gets 30 days to cure.
- Within 30 days of certification the Council holds a hearing. It then either adopts the ordinance without substantive amendment or submits it to the voters at an election within 180 days.
- Either way, the Council can't amend or repeal the result for a year.
- An area of the parish may petition, and the Council may call a referendum on its own motion (VI.A.2).

**Charter amendment by petition** (VIII.B-D)
- The Council specifies the form within 30 days.
- 15% of qualified electors must sign, and the petition is filed within 60 days.
- Substantially the same proposal can't go to the voters more than once every two years.

**Recall** (Charter VII.E points to state law: R.S. 18:1300.1 et seq.)
- The petition is directed to the governor, on the Secretary of State's approved form.
- The chairman files a copy with the Secretary of State before any signature is gathered.
- Signatures are handwritten. The share needed depends on the number of electors in the voting area: 40% under 1,000; 33⅓% from 1,000 to under 25,000; 25% from 25,000 to under 100,000; 20% at 100,000 or more (18:1300.2(B)(3)).
- The signed petition goes to the Registrar of Voters within 180 days of the filing (90 days where fewer than 1,000 electors live).
- The voting area is the official's own: one council district for a district member, the parish for an at-large member or the President. The Registrar certifies the elector count.

This is a summary of procedure, not advice on running a petition. Read the current sections before acting.
