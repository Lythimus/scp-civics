# Who decides what in St. Charles Parish

Checked 2026-11-17. This is not legal advice.

The Home Rule Charter is cited by article and section and paraphrased here. Its text stays out of this repository (see `scp-civics:scp-ordinances`). To read a section, run `python3 "${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/scp-ordinances/scripts/code_search.py" get "Charter IV B"`, and check the PDF page image before quoting it. The Council Rules and the statutes are quoted from Legistar and legis.la.gov.

People change. For names, use `skills/scp-meeting-record/references/officials.md` (generated by `meeting_record.py roster`) and check them against Legistar.

## The parish government

St. Charles has operated under a home rule charter since the Police Jury was replaced (Charter Art. I, IX). The Charter gives the parish every power not denied by the Charter, general law or the constitution, including general police power (Art. II).

**Parish Council** (Art. III.A)
- It has nine members. Seven are elected from single-member districts. Two are elected at large by the whole parish: the Division A member must live on the east bank and the Division B member on the west bank. So every address has three members, its district member and both at-large members.
- Terms are four years, concurrent with the governor's election. No member may serve a third consecutive full term in the same seat.
- Five members are a quorum. An action needs five votes, or six where two-thirds is required. Procedural matters pass by a majority of those present and voting (III.A.5.d).
- The Council elects a chairman and vice-chairman from its members. The Council Secretary gives notice of meetings, prepares the agenda and keeps the record (III.A.5.b), and is also the parish's custodian of public records (`scp-civics:scp-prr`).
- It holds all legislative power. It levies taxes, appropriates, creates and abolishes departments and special districts, and controls the budgets of boards it created. It can investigate any department or special district, with subpoenas (III.A.7).
- It fills its own vacancies within 30 days. If more than a year of the term remains, a special election follows (III.A.6).
- It may not dictate the appointment or removal of parish employees. Except for inquiry and investigation, it deals with them only through the President (III.C.6).
- The Charter sets at least monthly meetings. Council Rule 1 says the first and third Mondays at 6 PM, but the Council adopts an annual schedule each year that overrides it: the 2026 meetings fell on second and fourth Mondays in some months and first and third in others, with budget hearings on other days (Legistar, checked 2026-11-17). Use the year's schedule (a "Special Matter" titled "Adopt <year> Parish Council Meeting Schedule") or `bodies`.

**Parish President** (Art. III.B)
- The President is the chief executive, elected parishwide for the same term as the Council, with the same two-term limit.
- The President appoints department heads. Each needs Council confirmation by majority vote after a hearing within 30 days (III.B.3.a).
- The President attends Council meetings and may speak but doesn't vote. The President prepares the operating budget and capital program and must make monthly financial reports public (III.B.3.b).
- The President has a veto, including over single appropriation items (IV.C).

**Departments** (Art. III.C)
- Finance is created by the Charter. The finance director is ex officio treasurer and has custody of parish funds, except those of the School Board, the 29th Judicial District, the Sheriff and the Assessor (III.C.2.a.iv). That exception is a quick test of which offices are outside parish government.
- Planning and Zoning is created by the Charter (III.C.4).
- The District Attorney is the Council's legal advisor unless the Council creates a legal department (III.C.3). The code has a Department of Legal Services (Sec. 2-21).
- Other departments are created by ordinance in Chapter 2: community services, homeland security and emergency preparedness, public works, mosquito control, legal services, waterworks, economic development and tourism, and engineering (Secs. 2-17 to 2-24). Waterworks and Wastewater are parish departments, so their records go through the parish custodian.
- The Civil Service Board has five members chosen by different appointing authorities, and it hires the Personnel Officer (III.C.5).

## How an ordinance moves

An act with the force of law must be an ordinance (IV.A). That covers taxes, the budget, franchises, debt, conveying or leasing parish property, the zoning map and regulations, amending ordinances, Charter amendments and penalties. A resolution covers acts without the force of law, such as policy statements, authorizing a signature on an approved contract, and supporting a board's rules (IV.H).

1. **Introduction.** Only a Council member or the President can introduce an ordinance, at any meeting (IV.B.3.b; Rule 21). Rule 34 requires copies to the Council Secretary "by 4:00 o'clock p.m. on the sixth business day before the date of a Council Meeting".
2. **Publication.** Unless two-thirds reject it at once, the ordinance or a summary is published in the official journal with notice of the hearing at least a week ahead. It can't be adopted at the meeting where it was introduced unless it is an emergency ordinance (IV.B.3.b). The official journal is chosen under Sec. 2-3 and R.S. 43:141-149. The Herald Guide is the journal used for notices (`scp-civics:scp-land-use-process`).
3. **Hearing.** A summary is read and anyone interested may be heard (IV.B.3.c). The Council may amend, but not so as to nullify the ordinance's purpose (IV.B.3.g).
4. **Vote.** Passage takes a majority of members, five votes. Increasing a tax, service charge, occupational license fee or special fee takes two-thirds, six votes (IV.B.3.h). Each member's vote is recorded (IV.B.3.d). A member with a substantial personal financial interest must declare it and not vote (IV.B.3.e; Rule 22 lets the Council judge recusal).
5. **President.** The ordinance goes to the President within two days. The President has six calendar days to approve it or veto it with written reasons. If the President does neither, it takes effect (IV.C.1-2).
6. **Override.** At its next or following regular meeting, the Council can override a veto by two-thirds of all members (IV.C.3; Rule 35). Some acts can't be vetoed: Charter amendments, council procedure, council pay and staff, and acts required by petition or law (IV.C.4).
7. **Publication and effect.** The ordinance is published in full within ten days of approval or override. It takes effect no earlier than five days after publication (IV.B.3.d, f).

Other procedure:
- **Emergency ordinances** need two-thirds, may pass at introduction, and may take effect at once. They can't levy a tax or grant a franchise (IV.E).
- **Tabled matters.** Under Rule 8, tabled ordinances and resolutions "Shall only appear on two (2) subsequent regular meeting Agendas". After that they lapse with no vote, which Legistar labels "Council Rule 8" (`scp-civics:scp-decisions` reports these). Taking a matter from the table needs five votes, or six at the same meeting (Rule 28).
- **Meeting length.** Meetings end at 10 PM unless two-thirds extend them by an hour, and pending business carries to the next meeting (Rule 1).
- **Order of business** follows Rule 8. Any item can be taken out of order with five votes (Rule 25).
- **Budget** (Art. V). The President submits a balanced budget at least 75 days before January 1. The Council holds three public hearings, where any resident may speak, and adopts the budget at least 30 days before the year starts. If it fails to act, the President's budget takes effect. Details are in `scp-civics:scp-budget`.

The Council Rules are re-adopted at the organizational meeting of each term. The current rules are Legistar matter 2024-0004, adopted 9-0 on 2024-01-08. Its attachment is the text as proposed. The minutes record two floor amendments: a new Rule 40, which bars closing nominations until every member has had a chance to nominate, and new wording on addressing the Council (`participation.md`). No amendment since was found on Legistar on 2026-11-17. The minutes are "Final" in Legistar, though the PDF's header still reads "Draft". The next organizational meeting follows the 2027 election.

## Council committees

Rule 32 lets the Council create special committees by five votes. The Chairman names each committee's chair at the organizational meeting and each January. Committees are public bodies under R.S. 42:13(A)(3), and the parish must broadcast them live (R.S. 42:23(B)(2)(a)(i)).

## Offices and districts the Council doesn't control

The Charter leaves the School Board, the District Attorney, Sheriff, Clerk of Court, Assessor and Coroner, and the courts, outside its reach (Art. VII.G, paraphrased). Each office is elected separately and keeps its own records, so a parish records request doesn't reach them. State law also creates districts that reach into the parish:
