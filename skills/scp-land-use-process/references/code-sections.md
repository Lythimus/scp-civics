# St. Charles Parish code sections for land-use cases

These notes were read on Municode, Supplement 77 (updated 10/15/2026), on 2026-11-01. Before quoting, check Municode's "updated" date. If it has moved, re-read the section.

These are notes, not the verbatim text. Save a dated excerpt in the case folder for anything you quote: Appendix A Sec. XV, Charter Art. III §C.4, Appendix C, R.S. 33:101, 101.1, 106–109, 113, 4721, 4724, 4725, 4727 and 4780.40–.50, and R.S. 42:13, 14, 19 and 24.

To find or check code text, and for Municode node IDs, use `scp-civics:scp-ordinances`.

## Rezoning (Appendix A Sec. XV)

- **XV.A.2: the Council needs a verbatim transcript.** An amendment isn't effective unless the Council received the Commission's recommendation "contained within a verbatim transcript" of that part of the meeting.
- **XV.B: notice of the Commission hearing.**
  - **Sign:** on the property and at the Planning & Zoning office at least 10 days ahead.
  - **Certified mail:** to **abutting** owners at least 10 days ahead.
  - **Publication:** at least 3 times, on 3 separate weeks, in the official journal (the Herald-Guide), with at least **15 days** elapsed between the first publication and the hearing.
- **XV.D: the standard.** The request must meet "two or more" of three guidelines; guideline one is consistency with the Future Land Use Map.
- **XV.E: the Council's criteria.** Before the Council approves, there should be reasonable factual proof that the change complies with, or doesn't conflict with, the Comprehensive Plan and Future Land Use Map, and that it doesn't negatively affect health, safety and welfare.
- **XV.F: withdrawal.** From the Commission: written notice by noon on the Wednesday before the meeting, then no readvertising for six months unless the Commission votes otherwise. From the Council: written request to the Council Secretary before noon on the Thursday before the hearing.

## Variances (Appendix A Sec. XIII)

Read from the local copy of Supplement 77, codified through 2026-06-01, on 2026-11-02. Of the later Appendix A ordinances on Legistar that day, the titles of Ord. 26-8-14 (Sec. III, VI, VII) and Ord. 26-8-15 (Sec. XX) don't name Sec. XIII.

- **XIII.B: powers.** The Board hears appeals of administrative decisions and can vary building and structure rules for practical difficulties or unnecessary hardship. It has no authority to grant variances in a case that needs a special permit or special exception use approval.
- **XIII.C: meetings.** Third Thursday of the month in Council Chambers, with the following Thursday as the alternate if there is no quorum. It can cancel if no applications are scheduled. Four members are a quorum, and questions are decided by roll call.
- **XIII.C.7: filing.** The application, adjoining-owner form, supporting records and the Planning & Zoning rejection letter. The fee is $150 for single-family residential and $250 for everything else.
- **XIII.C.8: notice.** Sign on the property and at the Planning & Zoning office at least 10 days ahead; at least 3 publications on 3 separate weeks, with at least 15 days between the first and the hearing; certified mail to abutting owners at least 10 days ahead.
- **XIII.C.10: reapplication.** A denied request can't be refiled for the same property by the same parties.
- **XIII.D: criteria.** No variance as to the permitted use. The others include special conditions peculiar to the land, not caused by the applicant, no special privilege, no change to the locality's essential character, and demonstrable hardship. Financial hardship doesn't count.
- **XIII.E: findings.** Every grant or denial needs a written finding of fact based on sworn testimony and evidence. Conditions must be stated in the approval.
- **XIII.F: expiration.** One year, unless a building permit is obtained and substantial construction or the use has started. The Board can extend it for good cause.
- **XIII.G: appeal.** To the court of record under R.S. 33:4727.

- **Sec. 19-3:** the Council adopted a Comprehensive Land Use Plan by Ord. 90-8-2.

  - **2016 map update:** Commission on 2016-09-01, then Ord. 16-9-16 (Legistar 2016-0323, passed 2016-09-19), "to amend" the plan "to update the Future Land Use Map".
  - **2022 Update:** Commission Resolution 2022-1, then Council Resolution 6688 (Legistar 2023-0056, approved 2023-03-13), in support of "adopting the 10-year update".
  - **Rezonings that name the map:** the titles of Legistar 2021-0061 (failed) and 2021-0082 (enacted as Ord. 21-4-1) change both the zoning classification and the "Future Land Use Designation". Whether a Commission resolution accompanied either wasn't checked.
  - **Limits:** a title search finds only titles that name the map, and the Legistar API doesn't carry Commission matters. So this list doesn't establish how every map change was made.
- **Where it is:** the 2030 plan and its update are on the parish's Comprehensive Plan page as PDFs. A searchable Markdown conversion with page citations is in `scp-civics:scp-comprehensive-plan` (`corpus/STATUS.md` says what was checked). These notes don't summarize the plan; use that skill for what it says about a place, parcel or category.

Read the plan when:
- **A rezoning turns on the map.** XV.D.1 tests conformity with the Future Land Use Map. Find the site's designation yourself, and check it against what staff and the applicant say.
- **The Council is deciding.** XV.E.1 also covers the plan's "goals and recommendations", so the map alone doesn't answer it.
- **Someone cites the plan.** Check any quoted goal or claim of consistency against the text and page.
- **A use requires it.** App. A Sec. VII makes a Garden Home Development submit a statement of compliance with the plan's goals. Other uses may have similar rules, so search the code.
- **A map change is proposed.** Compare the proposed category with the update's map and what the plan says about that area.

A variance doesn't need it, because XIII.D doesn't cite the plan. A plat doesn't either, unless the record cites it. Check the update first, then the 2030 plan. Cite the document and page.

## Charter Art. III §C.4

- The planning commission is the parish zoning commission, "with all the powers, duties and functions" that state law gives zoning commissions.
- The director's master-plan work goes to the commission for review.
- No charter provision moves plan or Future Land Use Map amendments to the Council.

## State-law overlay

How these reach a home rule parish is covered in the next section.

- **R.S. 33:4780.45:** a zoning commission hearing needs at least 3 publications, with at least 10 days from the first publication to the hearing. Sec. XV.B is stricter.
- **R.S. 33:4780.43:** the Council's hearing needs publication once a week for 3 weeks, with at least 15 days of lead time, plus certified mail unless more than 10 parcels are involved.
- **R.S. 33:4780.44:** a protest by owners of 20% of the area, or of land within 200 ft, means the change needs "the favorable vote of a majority of the members" of the governing authority. That's a majority of members, not two-thirds.
- **R.S. 33:108(B)–(C):** a plan amendment needs a Commission hearing after one notice "of the purpose, time, and place" at least 10 days ahead, and adoption is "by resolution of a commission".

## State law on review, clocks and limits

Statutes were read on legis.la.gov on 2026-11-03. *Alexander* was read from lasc.org. The other cases were checked by docket or reporter cite, and their pin pages and writ histories weren't checked. This is research, not legal advice, so have a Louisiana lawyer check any deadline before relying on it. Look statutes up with `bayou:la-rs-search`. Without bayou, enter the title and section at https://legis.la.gov/legis/LawSearch.aspx.

**St. Charles is a home rule parish, and that limits all of this.** R.S. 33:4780.50(C) says the parish zoning Subpart (R.S. 33:4780.40-.50) doesn't change how a home rule parish structures and distributes its powers. Whether that Subpart's notice, vote and appeal rules still bind St. Charles wasn't established. So cite Appendix A or C first where it speaks, and name the statute as the state rule "if it applies". St. Charles isn't among the four parishes R.S. 33:4780.40 excludes (Rapides, Jefferson, Sabine, Webster), and how the law reaches those wasn't checked. Municipal rules (R.S. 33:4721-4729) are written for land inside a municipality.

**How a court starts.** *Alexander v. St. James Parish*, 2025-C-00866 (La. 3/6/26), vacated the Fifth Circuit's opinion (24-557). It deferred to the parish's reading of its own land-use plan and said "[t]here should be no de novo review". Don't cite the vacated opinion. *Alexander* came from another parish, and whether home rule changes its reasoning wasn't checked. *Tassin v. City of Marksville* (La. App. 3 Cir. 1982) held that failing to give notice the local ordinance required was fatal, but it's a municipal case that predates *Alexander*. So a counted notice shortfall under App. A is a better first argument than a dispute over what the code means. That's a reading of these cases, and no case read states it.

Also **not established:** whether a comment to the Commission is in the Council's record, whether a rezoning may carry conditions absent a code provision, and whether skipping a local appeal step bars a suit.

## Subdivision (Appendix C)

- **II.B, administrative resubdivision:** no net increase in lots, 5 lots or fewer and no public improvements. The Director and the Parish President sign it, with no notice and no hearing.
- **II.C, minor resubdivision:** a net increase of 5 lots or fewer and no new public improvements. No formal preliminary plat is required.
- **II.D, major subdivision:** the Commission approves the preliminary plat, the Director approves construction, and the Council gives final approval.
- **II.E.3, required submittals:** a Drainage Impact Analysis, a Traffic Impact Analysis, a stormwater pollution prevention plan (Ch. 25), a Drainage Impact Report and a sewer report.
- **II.E.4.b, hearing scheduled only after review:** the hearing is set only after the plat conforms **and** the Director of Public Works and Wastewater has approved the Drainage Impact Analysis and the Traffic Impact Analysis.
- **II.E.4.c and V.B.1, waivers:** a waiver needs "impracticable" or "undue hardship because of peculiar conditions pertaining to the land". Financial hardship doesn't count, and the waiver must not be "detrimental to the public interest". It takes the Commission plus a supporting Council resolution.
- **II.E.4.d, plat hearing notice:** the same as Sec. XV.B (sign 10 days ahead, 3 publications over 3 weeks, 15 days of lead time). Certified mail goes to the owners of land **adjoining** the plat, or the next landowner over if the subdivider owns the adjoining land, and to the Council Secretary.
- **II.E.4.f, the decision:** approve, approve with written conditions, or disapprove with written reasons within 60 days. An approval is valid for 6 months.
- **Construction approval, wetlands:** a Corps-approved wetland delineation and all wetland permits before construction plan approval, plus written sign-off by the parish Coastal Zone Management Section.
- **Sec. IV, roadside drainage:** must be subsurface. An open-swale or open-ditch waiver needs a formal letter, acceptance letters from both the Public Works and the Planning & Zoning Directors, the Commission and a supporting Council resolution.
- **Sec. IV.5, the drainage impact analysis:**
  - It must be "a comprehensive drainage impact analysis of all proposed subdivision and surrounding affected areas", submitted before plat approval.
  - Design storm: 25-year, 24-hour.
  - Post-development peak flow must be at or below pre-development peak flow.
  - It must include an area drainage map and the capacity downstream "to the first public outfall, coulee, canal or river".
  - The analysis can be waived only by Council **ordinance**.
- **V.A.2, fee:** an industrial-area or planned-industrial-park plat costs $100 plus $100 an acre, capped at $5,000.
- **V.B.2, later resubdivision:** once a planned industrial park or industrial development is approved, it can be **resubdivided by the Director's and Parish President's signatures alone**, with no hearing. That makes the original plat hearing the last public look at how the lots are laid out.

## Counting notice days

"At least 15 days shall elapse between the first publication and the date of the hearing." Thursday notices on 9/24, 10/1 and 10/8 for a 10/8 hearing count 14 days by plain subtraction, yet the parish uses this pattern routinely. Raise it as a question, not a defect. For a hearing on day H, the latest Thursday that leaves 15 days by subtraction is H − 21 when H is a Thursday.
