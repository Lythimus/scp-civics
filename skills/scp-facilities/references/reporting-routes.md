# Reporting routes for St. Charles Parish

Every number was read on the source named, as of 2026-11-03. Numbers change, so check the source before relying on one. `stcharlesparish.gov` returns 403 to scripts; open it in a browser.

"NRC" can mean the National Response Center or the Nuclear Regulatory Commission, so write the full name.

**A resident isn't the one legally required to notify.** LDEQ says a call to LDEQ doesn't satisfy a facility's emergency notification. That duty is the facility's. A resident's report is a record that someone noticed something, at a place and time.

## LDEQ complaints

- **Online form:** [File a Citizen Complaint](https://internet.deq.louisiana.gov/portal/ONLINESERVICES/FORMS/FILE-A-CITIZEN-COMPLAINT). It requires name, phone, email, the alleged violator's address, date, time the problem began, parish and a description. It can ask for a follow-up on the inspector's findings, and takes up to 5 attachments of 3 MB each.
- **Anonymous:** the form says not to use it for anonymous complaints. Those go by phone, 225-219-3640. LDEQ's complaint policy says anonymous complaints "often lack the necessary information".
- **Email:** SPOC@la.gov. The SPOC page also lists 225-342-1234.
- **Referred elsewhere:** LDEQ's policy sends agriculture, drinking water and indoor air complaints to other agencies.
- **Criminal concerns:** LDEQ has a separate Criminal Investigation Division form.
- **Your details can become public.** LDEQ posts an incident's file to EDMS, which anyone can search. One online complaint's file, checked 2026-11-03, reproduced the form with the complainant's name, phone, mailing address and email, unredacted. Assume a named complaint will be posted the same way. The way around this is an anonymous call to LDEQ's public line, 225-219-3640, weekdays 8:00-4:30. How the parish EOC or the State Police handle a caller's details is not established.
- **How long it takes.** The same complaint, filed online on a Saturday night, got an email from LDEQ's regional office on the Monday, and its file was entered in EDMS about eight weeks after filing. A resident reports that phone and email complaints usually take weeks to months to process. These are single experiences, not LDEQ's stated timelines.
- **Numbers.** The online form returns a confirmation number (OC####). The incident gets its own tracking number (T-######), which is the one that labels the file in EDMS, so ask for it.
- **Finding the file.** EDMS can't search by tracking number, because its keyword filter has no effect. Search the document list by the source facility's AI number and the incident date, then read the descriptions for the T number. In the file checked, the description was the T number, the function was "Incidents - Non-Emergency", and it was indexed under the source's AIs, not the complainant's. Downloading a document means passing a reCAPTCHA, which the person does themselves.

## What the standards say

From LAC 33:III ([Part III text](https://www.doa.la.gov/media/q4eanemv/33v03.docx)). These are regulators' tests, and a resident can't establish them by eye or nose. Drafts record what was seen and smelled, and never say a standard was broken.

- **§1101, smoke:** not darker than 20% average opacity, except one six-minute period in any 60 minutes.
- **§1105, flares:** smoke not over 20% opacity for a combined 6 hours in any 10 consecutive days. No rule was found limiting how long a flare may burn.
- **§1106:** opacity is read by Reference Method 9.
- **§2901, odor:** perceived intensity of 6 or more on the 8-point butanol scale at or beyond the property line, measured by Method 41, with exemptions.

An inspector can only measure an event still going on, which is the reason to report during it. The parish code: no odor or flare rule for plants was found by search; check with `scp-civics:scp-ordinances`.
